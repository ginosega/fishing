## Overview

Topwater fishing is the surface branch of [Bass Fishing Techniques](kb://bass-fishing-techniques): instead of trying to reach fish at a particular underwater depth, you are asking a bass to **rise and attack at the surface**. It is most effective when bass are looking up, using shallow cover, chasing baitfish, or willing to react to a surface disturbance.

Do not reduce topwater to “dawn and dusk only.” Low light is a high-percentage starting point, but good surface fishing can continue through overcast weather, wind, shade, schooling activity, or heavy vegetation that creates overhead cover. A frog over a grass mat at midday is a very different presentation from a popper on a calm point at sunrise, but both are topwater.

Use [Bass Behavior and Habitat](kb://bass-behavior-and-habitat) to decide **where bass are likely to be**. Use this page to decide **whether they are likely to feed upward, which topwater style fits the cover and conditions, how to work it, and how to respond to a strike or miss**.

## Topwater Quick Start

| Situation | Start With | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calm or lightly rippled water, isolated targets | [Popper](kb://technique-popper) | Can stay in the strike zone and make a subtle pop-pause presentation |
| Open water, points, weed edges, schooling bait | [Walking Bait](kb://technique-walking-bait) | Covers water while gliding side to side like a fleeing or injured baitfish |
| Wind/chop, stained water, active fish | [Whopper Plopper](kb://technique-whopper-plopper) or [Buzzbait](kb://technique-buzzbait) | More sound, vibration, and continuous surface disturbance |
| Lily pads, mats, slop, reeds, heavy vegetation | [Frog](kb://technique-frogs) | Weedless presentation can cross cover where exposed treble hooks cannot |
| Docks, wood, shade, pockets in vegetation | Popper or frog | Can be kept close to a target and paused |
| Bass visibly chasing bait | Walking bait or Whopper Plopper | Matches a fleeing-baitfish pattern and lets you cover the feeding zone quickly |
| Fish boil but miss the lure | Pause first; then follow with a soft plastic | A missed topwater strike often identifies an exact fish location |

## When Topwater Works

### Surface Clues Matter More Than the Clock

Good signs include:

- baitfish dimpling, spraying, or fleeing at the surface;
- bass waking, swirling, or busting bait;
- bluegill or other forage concentrated around shallow cover;
- insects or other surface food drawing activity;
- birds working bait;
- fish holding beneath mats, pads, docks, shade, or overhanging cover;
- warm-season low-light periods when bass move shallow to feed.

If you can **see the food or the predators using the surface**, topwater deserves serious consideration even outside the classic first-light or last-light window.

### Light

Topwater is often easiest to start in:

- early morning;
- evening;
- overcast weather;
- shaded banks;
- docks and overhanging trees;
- wind-broken or stained water where fish feel less exposed.

Bright sun does not automatically end the bite. In summer, a bass can remain shallow all day if a grass mat, dock, laydown, or dark shade line gives it security. See [Summer Fishing](kb://summer-fishing).

### Season

- **Spring:** As water warms and bass move shallow, topwater opportunities increase. Post-spawn fish, fry-guarders, and recovering fish can become especially willing to strike upward. See [Spring Fishing](kb://spring-fishing).
- **Summer:** Usually the most consistent season for frogs, walking baits, poppers, buzzbaits, and plopper-style baits, especially in low light, vegetation, and shade. See [Summer Fishing](kb://summer-fishing).
- **Fall:** Surface feeding can become excellent when bass chase baitfish on flats, points, wind-blown banks, or into coves. Match the moving forage and stay mobile. See [Fall Fishing](kb://fall-fishing).
- **Winter:** Topwater is normally a low-percentage starting choice, but mild weather can create brief shallow or surface activity. Follow what the fish are actually doing rather than a calendar rule. See [Winter Fishing](kb://winter-fishing).

Water temperature is a useful clue, but it is not an on/off switch. Forage position, light, cover, wind, and actual surface activity are better evidence than a single temperature number.

### Wind and Surface Texture

A little surface disturbance can help by reducing visibility and making bass more comfortable roaming.

- **Calm:** popper, subtle walking bait, natural colors, longer pauses.
- **Light ripple:** walking bait, popper, Whopper Plopper.
- **Moderate chop:** louder walking bait, Whopper Plopper, buzzbait.
- **Heavy chop:** topwater becomes harder for both angler and fish to track; a subsurface spinnerbait or chatterbait may be more efficient.

## Where to Fish Topwater

### Vegetation

Topwater is especially useful around:

- lily pads;
- matted grass;
- emergent reeds;
- duckweed or surface slop;
- submerged grass that stops just below the surface;
- holes and lanes in vegetation;
- outside weed edges.

Use a [Frog](kb://technique-frogs) when exposed hooks would constantly foul. Over submerged grass or cleaner edges, a walking bait, popper, or Whopper Plopper may cover water faster.

### Docks, Wood, and Shade

Bass often use the darkest available shade and the first hard cover beside deeper water.

Try:

- skipping a frog under overhangs;
- popping beside a dock post and letting the lure sit;
- walking a bait parallel to a dock face;
- running a buzzbait or plopper past laydowns and dock corners;
- making repeated casts to the best piece of shade instead of treating every foot of bank equally.

### Points, Flats, and Weed Edges

Walking baits and plopper-style baits are strong search tools over:

- points;
- shallow flats beside deep water;
- long weed edges;
- rocky shorelines;
- submerged grass;
- the ends of docks;
- open-water feeding zones.

When bass are chasing bait, cast **past** the activity and bring the lure through it rather than landing directly on the fish.

### Schooling Fish

If bass are visibly busting bait:

1. approach quietly;
2. cast beyond or to the edge of the school;
3. work a walking bait or plopper through the feeding zone;
4. keep watching for the school to reappear;
5. have a follow-up bait ready if fish refuse the surface lure.

A small swimbait or soft jerkbait is a strong follow-up when bass are still feeding but stop committing to topwater.

## Choosing a Topwater Style

### Walking Bait

See [Walking Bait](kb://technique-walking-bait).

Best for open water, points, weed edges, docks, schooling fish, and situations where you need to cover water.

Key idea: **slack creates the walk**.

- Point the rod tip down or to the side.
- Twitch the rod with short snaps.
- Give the lure enough slack to glide left and right.
- Pick up only the excess line.
- Vary cadence until fish show a preference.

Use a slower walk with pauses when fish are hesitant. Speed up when fish are aggressively chasing bait.

### Popper

See [Popper](kb://technique-popper).

Best around isolated targets, calm water, docks, wood, pockets, and situations where you want the lure to stay in one place.

A useful cadence is:

1. pop;
2. pause;
3. allow the surface rings to fade;
4. pop again.

Do not assume every retrieve needs the same rhythm. Try single pops, double pops, longer dead-sticks, and short walking sequences.

My Gear: [Rebel Pop-R](gear://rebel-pop-r)

### Whopper Plopper / Prop-Style Topwater

See [Whopper Plopper](kb://technique-whopper-plopper).

Use when you want a continuous, easily tracked surface disturbance and the ability to cover water.

Good places include:

- rocky shorelines;
- points;
- dock edges;
- submerged vegetation;
- open water over shallow cover;
- schooling fish.

Start with a steady retrieve, then experiment with short speed changes or pauses. Keep the tail/prop working cleanly; weeds on the rear hardware can kill the action.

My Gear: [River2Sea Whopper Plopper 60](gear://river2sea-whopper-plopper-60)

### Buzzbait

See [Buzzbait](kb://technique-buzzbait).

A buzzbait is a faster, single-hook search bait for warm water, shallow cover, wind/chop, low light, and stained water.

- Begin retrieving immediately after splashdown.
- Reel just fast enough to keep it planing on the surface.
- Run it close to wood, grass edges, docks, and other cover.
- If fish follow, change speed or add a brief stop-and-go variation.
- A trailer can add lift, bulk, and a larger target.

Because the hook is exposed, a buzzbait is less weedless than a hollow-body frog but can be easier to hook fish on in sparse cover.

### Frog

See [Frog](kb://technique-frogs).

Use a frog when vegetation or shallow cover makes other topwaters impractical.

Strong frog water includes:

- lily pads;
- grass mats;
- reeds;
- duckweed/slop;
- holes in vegetation;
- shade beneath overhanging cover;
- shallow wood and docks.

A hollow-body frog can also be walked in open water, so do not treat it as a vegetation-only bait.

## Tackle and Line

Match tackle to the **hook style and cover**, not merely to the fact that the lure floats.

### Treble-Hook Topwaters

For walking baits, poppers, and Whopper Plopper-style lures:

- Rod: roughly 6'6"–7'2", medium or medium-heavy; enough tip to work the lure and enough give to keep treble hooks pinned.
- Reel: medium to fast retrieve, especially for walking baits where you repeatedly pick up slack.
- Line: monofilament is an excellent open-water default because it floats and adds stretch; braid can be useful for long casts or heavier cover.
- Avoid a fluorocarbon main line for most surface lures because it sinks and can pull the nose of the bait down or change its action.

See [Fishing Line](kb://technique-fishing-line).

If using braid with a treble-hook topwater in open water, a short mono leader can add some stretch and keep the terminal connection floating. In heavy cover, direct braid may be preferable.

### Frogs

For hollow-body frogs in heavy vegetation:

- Rod: about 7'–7'6", heavy, fast.
- Reel: high-speed baitcaster.
- Line: 50–65 lb braid, typically direct to the frog.
- Knot: [Palomar Knot](kb://knot-palomar).

The goal is not finesse; it is to drive heavy hooks home and move the fish out of cover.

### Buzzbaits

A medium-heavy to heavy baitcasting setup with braid is a strong starting point. Tie directly to the bait rather than adding a snap-swivel that can interfere with the wire frame or collect vegetation.

See [Snaps & Swivels](kb://technique-snaps-swivels).

## Color, Size, and Sound

Topwater color is mostly about **silhouette, visibility, and forage match**.

Useful starting points:

- **White / shad / bone:** general baitfish imitation.
- **Natural or translucent:** clear water and bright conditions.
- **Black or other dark colors:** strong silhouette in low light, stained water, or against a bright sky.
- **Bluegill/perch patterns:** useful where those fish are major forage.
- **Chartreuse or louder accents:** stained water or when you want more visibility.

When deciding between quiet and loud:

- use a subtle popper or natural walking bait when fish are pressured or the surface is calm;
- use rattles, a plopper, popping frog, or buzzbait when you need to call fish through wind, stain, or distance.

Size should roughly match forage, but do not be afraid to change profile if fish follow without committing.

## Retrieve Strategy

Before changing lures, change the retrieve.

Try:

- faster cadence;
- slower cadence;
- longer pause;
- no pause;
- repeated casts to one target;
- a different casting angle;
- a stop immediately beside cover;
- a sudden speed burst followed by a kill.

Topwater often produces visible feedback. A follow, swirl, boil, or short strike is information: the fish may want a different cadence rather than a different lure.

## Strikes and Hooksets

The most common topwater mistake is reacting to the **sight** of the strike instead of the **feel** of the fish.

### Treble-Hook Baits

For poppers, walking baits, and Whopper Ploppers:

1. do not jerk immediately when the water explodes;
2. keep enough tension to stay connected;
3. wait until the rod loads or you feel the fish;
4. sweep firmly and keep pressure on it.

An explosive hookset can pull small treble hooks away before they are loaded.

### Frog

A frog requires a different response because of the heavy double hook.

- Let the fish take the frog.
- Reel down quickly.
- When you feel the fish's weight, set hard.
- Keep the fish moving out of vegetation.

If you repeatedly miss frog strikes, first confirm you are waiting for weight before setting. Then check hook position, body softness, and leg length as described on the [Frog](kb://technique-frogs) page.

### Buzzbait

Keep reeling until the rod begins to load, then sweep firmly. Do not stop the bait and throw slack immediately at the first splash.

## Missed Strikes and Follow-Up Baits

A miss is not a failed cast; it gives you an exact target.

First:

- pause the topwater if it is still in the strike zone;
- twitch it once or twice;
- give the fish a chance to come back.

If it does not return, immediately cast a slower follow-up to the same spot:

- [Wacky Worm](kb://technique-wacky-worm);
- weightless soft jerkbait / fluke;
- [Ned Rig](kb://technique-ned-rig) around rock or open bottom;
- jig or other soft plastic around cover.

This is especially effective when the fish showed itself but did not fully commit.

## Bank Fishing

From shore:

- fish **parallel to the bank** before casting straight out;
- work shade lines, overhanging trees, docks, reeds, weed edges, and points;
- make long casts across shallow flats at low light;
- use a frog where shoreline vegetation makes treble-hook baits impractical;
- avoid walking directly to the water's edge before fishing the closest cover.

A bass can be closer to shore than you expect.

## Kayak Fishing

A kayak is excellent for topwater because it lets you control casting angle and approach quietly.

- Stay outside a weed edge and cast parallel to it.
- Fan-cast points and flats instead of making only one angle.
- Use the kayak to reach the shaded side of docks or overhanging cover.
- Let wind create a controlled drift along a productive bank or edge.
- Keep a follow-up rod ready after a missed blowup.
- Be careful with treble-hook fish beside the kayak; control the fish before reaching for the lure, and use pliers when practical.
