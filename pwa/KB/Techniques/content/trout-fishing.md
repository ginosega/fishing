## Overview

Use [Trout Behavior and Habitat](kb://trout-behavior-and-habitat) to decide **where trout are likely to be and what depth they are using**. This page starts with the next question: **once you have located likely trout, what presentation gives you the best chance of catching them?**

Choose a presentation by working through four variables:

1. **Depth:** Are trout feeding at the surface, cruising shallow, suspended in open water, or holding near bottom?
2. **Feeding mode:** Are they chasing baitfish, taking insects, or picking food near bottom?
3. **Access:** Are you fishing from bank, kayak, or moving water?
4. **Activity level:** Are fish visibly feeding and willing to chase, or do you need a slower/stationary presentation?

A useful default is to **match depth before changing lure**. If a reasonable lure is running five feet above or below the fish, changing color may accomplish nothing.

## Choosing a Presentation

| Situation | Good Starting Choice | Adjustment if Needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trout rising / surface activity | Small spoon or [Inline Spinner](kb://technique-inline-spinner); small fly where practical | Slow down, reduce size/flash, or cast ahead of rises |
| Cool water / shallow cruisers | Inline spinner, Kastmaster/spoon, small jerkbait or crankbait | Fan-cast and vary retrieve depth |
| Clear water / spooky fish | Small natural spoon/spinner, subtle minnow lure | Longer casts, lighter leader, slower retrieve |
| Wind/chop / active fish | Spinner or spoon with enough weight for control | Increase flash/contrast if visibility is reduced |
| Suspended trout | Counted-down spoon, crankbait, or troll at exact fish depth | Change weight/line length before changing lure |
| Deep summer fish | Weighted trolling rig, deeper crankbait/spoon, or bait just off bottom | Find the comfortable depth band with sonar |
| Bank fishing, unknown depth | [Slip Sinker Rig](kb://technique-slip-sinker-rig) plus active casting | Shorten/lengthen leader or recast to different depth |
| Fish shallow or visible from bank | [Bobber Rig](kb://technique-bobber-rig) | Adjust bait depth until it intersects fish |
| Covering lake water by kayak | [Inline Trolling Rig](kb://technique-inline-trolling-rig) | Add/remove weight and repeat productive depth |
| Fish need extra attraction while trolling | [Flasher Rig](kb://technique-flasher-rig) / dodger setup | Shorten leader for more action; lengthen for subtler action |
| Stream or river trout | Inline spinner, small spoon, drifted bait/fly where legal | Work seams, pools, current breaks, and different angles |

## Casting Lures

### Spoons and Kastmasters

Spoons are excellent search lures because they cast far, cover several depths, and imitate small baitfish or other flashing prey.

Good starting approach:

1. Cast beyond the target area.
2. Let the spoon sink to the desired depth.
3. Retrieve steadily with occasional pauses or speed changes.
4. If trout follow but do not strike, add a pause or slow the retrieve before changing lures.

Use a heavier spoon when you need casting distance, wind control, or more depth. The 1/4 oz Kastmaster reaches deeper water more easily than the 1/8 oz size.

A spoon can also be tipped with a small piece of nightcrawler or PowerBait where bait is legal and you intend to fish under the applicable bait rules.

My Gear: [Kastmaster](gear://kastmaster), [Dick Nite Spoon](gear://dick-nite-spoon)

### Inline Spinners

See [Inline Spinner](kb://technique-inline-spinner).

Spinners combine flash and vibration and are especially useful around creek mouths, rocky edges, shaded banks, current seams, and active shallow fish.

- Start the blade immediately after the cast with a small rod-tip pop if needed.
- Retrieve only fast enough to keep the blade turning cleanly.
- In lakes, count the spinner down before retrieving when fish are deeper.
- In streams, cast upstream or quartering across current and guide the lure past seams, rocks, and cover.
- Use a swivel in the rig as described on the Inline Spinner page to manage line twist.

My Gear: [Mepps Aglia #3](gear://mepps-aglia-3)

### Small Crankbaits and Jerkbaits

Small minnow-shaped lures are useful when trout are feeding on juvenile fish or are willing to chase.

- **Crankbait:** use a steady retrieve to hold a repeatable depth; especially useful for trolling or covering water.
- **Jerkbait:** twitch-pause retrieves can trigger following fish; lengthen pauses in colder water.
- **Floating minnow:** can be worked over shallow water or allowed to rise over cover.

See [Crankbait](kb://technique-crankbait) and [Jerkbait](kb://technique-jerkbait).

## Still Fishing from Shore

### Slip Sinker Rig

See [Slip Sinker Rig](kb://technique-slip-sinker-rig).

This is a strong choice when trout are near bottom or when you want a stationary presentation in deeper reachable water.

- Use enough leader for floating bait to rise above weeds or bottom debris.
- Cast, let the sinker settle, and reel only enough to remove excess slack.
- Watch for light taps, a slowly tightening line, repeated tip movement, or the rod steadily loading.
- If nothing happens after roughly 10–15 minutes, check the bait and change distance, angle, or leader length rather than leaving the same unproductive presentation indefinitely.

My Gear: [Berkley PowerBait Trout Dough](gear://berkley-powerbait-trout-dough), [Berkley PowerBait Trout Nuggets](gear://berkley-powerbait-trout-nuggets), [Berkley Magnum Floating Power Eggs](gear://berkley-powerbait-magnum-floating-power-eggs)

### Bobber Rig

See [Bobber Rig](kb://technique-bobber-rig).

A bobber is useful when trout are shallow, visibly cruising, feeding above weeds, or when you need to hold bait at a known depth.

- Start around 3–5 feet deep when fish are shallow.
- Go deeper if there are no bites and deeper water is nearby.
- Keep the bait above weed tops rather than burying it in vegetation.
- Cast near shade lines, weed edges, dock edges, shoreline drop-offs, visible fish activity, and mildly wind-blown water.
- Give the bobber an occasional small twitch, then let the bait settle.

A slip bobber is preferable when the productive depth becomes too deep for a fixed float.

## Trolling from a Kayak

Trolling turns depth control into a repeatable search tool.

### Inline Trolling Rig

See [Inline Trolling Rig](kb://technique-inline-trolling-rig).

Use this when you want a simple weighted presentation behind the kayak.

- Morning/evening or shallow fish: start light, around 1/4–1/2 oz.
- Deeper fish: increase weight only enough to reach and hold the target depth.
- Troll 70–120 feet behind the kayak as a starting range when space allows.
- Make gentle S-turns; the inside lure slows and falls while the outside lure speeds and rises.
- After a strike, repeat the same route, depth, and turn direction before making major changes.

My Gear: [Mack's Wedding Ring](gear://macks-wedding-ring)

### Flasher / Dodger Rig

See [Flasher Rig](kb://technique-flasher-rig).

Use a dodger when extra flash and lure action may help draw fish.

- Keep the leader behind the dodger short enough to transfer action, but lengthen it when fish seem wary.
- Tip a Wedding Ring or similar lure with a small bait piece where legal.
- Add weight to reach deeper fish rather than simply letting out unlimited line.
- If the rig stops producing, first change depth and speed before changing every component.

### Trolling Depth Strategy

Depth matters more than the exact route when trout are suspended.

- If sonar shows fish at 15 feet over 40 feet of water, target **15 feet**, not bottom.
- If marks are scattered from 8–20 feet, make passes at several controlled depths.
- If fish are consistently below your lure, add weight or slow enough to let the rig sink.
- If fish are consistently above your lure, lighten the rig or shorten line.

When you hook a fish, note the depth, nearby structure, speed, and whether the kayak was turning. Try to reproduce those variables.

## Streams and Rivers

For river trout, presentation is largely about putting the lure or bait through the feeding lane without making the fish fight unnecessary current.

Work:

- seams between fast and slow water;
- heads and tails of pools;
- pockets behind boulders;
- undercut or shaded banks;
- deeper runs;
- tributary mouths.

With a spinner or spoon, cast upstream or quartering across current so the lure works naturally past the holding spot. Keep enough tension to feel the lure but avoid dragging it unnaturally across the current.

Move methodically. A short cast through the correct seam is usually better than a long cast through featureless fast water.

## Water Clarity, Light, and Color

| Conditions | Starting Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Clear / bright | Natural silver, copper, olive, or baitfish colors; smaller profile; longer casts; fish deeper/shade |
| Clear / low light | Natural colors with enough flash to be noticed; check shallower water |
| Light stain | Gold, brighter metallic finishes, moderate contrast |
| Stained / dark | Gold, chartreuse, black/bright contrast, glow or UV where appropriate |
| Calm / spooky fish | Smaller lure, subtler finish, slower retrieve, lighter leader |
| Wind / chop | Slightly heavier lure for control; spinner/spoon with strong flash or vibration |

Color is secondary to **depth, speed, and getting the lure into the feeding lane**. Change those first.

## Bank Fishing Workflow

A practical bank sequence:

1. **Look before casting.** Check for rises, cruising fish, shade, inflows, weed edges, points, and drop-offs.
2. **Fish close water first.** Trout can cruise near shore, especially in cool or low-light conditions.
3. **Fan cast with a spoon or spinner.** Work left, straight out, and right at more than one depth.
4. **Put out a still-fishing rig.** Use a slip sinker for bottom/deep water or a bobber for shallow/suspended fish.
5. **Move if the area is dead.** Change angle or location rather than repeatedly casting the same empty water.
6. **Repeat what produces.** A bite at a particular depth, shade line, or wind exposure is a pattern clue.

Early morning and evening are usually the best bank windows. Midday can still produce if you can reach shade, deeper water, an inflow, or an active hatch.

## Kayak Fishing and Electronics

A kayak makes it much easier to follow trout vertically.

Use sonar to find:

- suspended fish;
- bait schools;
- thermocline-like depth bands;
- steep shoreline breaks;
- points and submerged structure;
- weed tops;
- the depth where fish repeatedly appear.

Do not chase every individual sonar mark. First identify a **repeatable depth or area**.

When fish are suspended, trolling is often more efficient than repeatedly casting. When fish are concentrated near a point, inflow, or rise activity, stop covering water and cast to them.

If the fish finder contradicts a general seasonal rule, follow the fish finder.

## Detecting Strikes and Setting the Hook

Trout often have relatively soft mouths, especially compared with bass. Avoid excessively hard hooksets.

- **Spoon / spinner / crankbait / jerkbait:** keep reeling until the rod loads, then sweep smoothly.
- **Trolling:** let the rod load under the fish before taking it from the holder or making a controlled sweep.
- **Bobber:** if it slides, dips, or disappears, reel down and lift firmly rather than snapping hard.
- **Slip sinker / bait:** when the line steadily tightens or the rod loads, reel down and make a firm lift.

Maintain steady pressure during the fight. Sudden slack is one of the easiest ways to lose a trout on small hooks.

## Handling and Regulations

Before each trip, check the current rules for the exact water. Washington trout waters vary in season, bait rules, hook restrictions, retention, and two-pole eligibility.

If releasing trout:

- land them efficiently;
- wet your hands before touching them;
- keep them in the water as much as practical;
- use single/barbless hooks where required or when release is the goal.

Bait can increase deep-hooking risk, so it is a better choice when legal and consistent with your harvest/release plan.

Current regulations and lake-specific information are available through [WDFW fishing regulations and Fish Washington](https://wdfw.wa.gov/fishing/regulations).
