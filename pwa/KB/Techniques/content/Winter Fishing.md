## Overview

Winter rewards precision more than coverage. Cold water slows bass metabolism and often concentrates them around stable structure, while trout may have access to a much broader vertical range because many lakes are mixed rather than strongly stratified.

The important distinction is that **cold does not mean every fish is on the deepest bottom**. Bass often use the deepest stable habitat available near forage and hard structure, but can make short moves shallower during mild warming periods. Trout can remain surprisingly high or near shore in cold mixed water, even though their feeding rate may be lower than in spring or fall.

Use this page for the seasonal question: **where is the most stable, food-rich winter habitat, and how slowly or precisely should I fish it?** For underlying habitat principles, see [Bass Behavior and Habitat](kb://bass-behavior-and-habitat) and [Trout Behavior and Habitat](kb://trout-behavior-and-habitat). For lure mechanics and rigging, see [Bass Fishing Techniques](kb://bass-fishing-techniques) and [Trout Fishing Techniques](kb://technique-trout-fishing).

## Bass: Where to Find Them

### Stable Wintering Structure

Bass generally reduce unnecessary movement in cold water, so start with places that provide depth, hard structure, and access to forage without forcing fish to travel far.

High-percentage winter areas include:

- steep rocky points;
- channel edges and channel swings;
- humps and ledges;
- deep rock piles;
- gravel-to-rock or other hard-bottom transitions;
- deep ends of docks or wood where sufficient depth exists;
- the last healthy vegetation near deeper water;
- areas where baitfish repeatedly appear on sonar.

Smallmouth often group tightly on rock or offshore structure. Finding one fish can mean several more are nearby. Largemouth may also concentrate, but some remain around vegetation or shallow cover when local conditions remain stable and forage is present.

### Depth Is Relative

“Deep” means deep relative to the water the fish normally use, not necessarily the deepest hole in the lake.

A 12-foot break may be wintering water in a shallow pond; a large clear lake may require much deeper structure. Let these clues define the winter zone:

- repeated fish/bait marks;
- steep access to deeper water;
- stable hard bottom;
- known summer/fall routes converging on the area;
- fish grouped rather than scattered.

### Mild Warming Periods

Several mild sunny days can create short feeding opportunities shallower than the main winter position.

Check:

- sun-exposed rock;
- dark-bottom pockets with nearby depth;
- protected banks out of cold wind;
- shallow cover directly connected to a steep break.

Treat these as **feeding excursions**, not proof that the entire winter population relocated.

## Bass: How to Catch Them

### Slow Enough to Stay in the Zone

Cold-water bass often will not travel far to intercept a lure. The goal is to keep a believable presentation near them.

Strong choices:

- **Ned rig:** drag, dead-stick, or make tiny hops on rock/gravel.
- **Drop shot:** hold the bait at fish depth with very little movement.
- **Finesse/football jig:** crawl slowly across hard bottom.
- **Suspending jerkbait:** use long pauses and allow the lure to sit in the strike zone.
- **Small swimbait:** slow-roll just above bottom or through suspended fish.

A winter retrieve that feels almost too slow can be appropriate.

### Do Not Confuse Inactivity with Absence

If sonar or prior bites show fish are present:

1. change casting angle;
2. reduce speed;
3. lengthen pauses;
4. downsize the profile;
5. put the lure closer to their exact depth;
6. only then abandon the area.

Winter is a poor season for repeatedly leaving known fish to search random water.

### Best Daily Window

Winter bass activity may improve during the warmest, brightest part of a mild day rather than at dawn. Midday/afternoon warming can be more useful than an early-morning start, especially in protected shallow-to-mid-depth areas.

## Trout: Where to Find Them

### Mixed Water Changes the Summer Rule

Many lowland lakes lose strong summer stratification as they cool and mix. When that happens, trout are no longer forced into the same narrow temperature/oxygen band.

Look for:

- forage rather than a fixed thermocline depth;
- shoreline breaks and points;
- creek channels and deeper basins where food gathers;
- inflows/outlets and mild current;
- fish suspended anywhere in the water column;
- midday surface or nearshore activity during mild conditions.

The absence of a summer thermocline means **re-scan the whole water column** instead of automatically returning to summer depths.

### Resident Coastal Cutthroat

In Lake Washington, research found coastal cutthroat generally shallower during winter mixing than during summer stratification. Local spawning movements can also reduce availability in some waters during late winter as adults use tributaries, so lake-specific seasonal prospects can differ even when the water itself is comfortable.

### Stocked Rainbow Trout

Winter opportunity varies widely by lake because stocking schedules, seasonal closures, and harvest history differ. Check current WDFW regulations and plant reports before assuming a lowland lake is open or recently stocked.

## Trout: How to Catch Them

### Slow Casting

When trout are feeding but not chasing aggressively:

- use a small spoon or [Inline Spinner](kb://technique-inline-spinner);
- slow the retrieve while keeping the lure working properly;
- add pauses to a small minnow lure;
- make repeated casts through the same productive depth/feeding lane;
- use natural/subtle colors in clear water.

If fish are visible near the surface on a mild day, do not fish beneath them simply because it is winter.

### Still Fishing

A [Slip Sinker Rig](kb://technique-slip-sinker-rig) can be effective when trout are near bottom or when a stationary presentation is the easiest way to reach stable deeper water. A [Bobber Rig](kb://technique-bobber-rig) remains useful when trout are suspended or feeding shallow.

Adjust depth before changing bait repeatedly.

### Slow Trolling

Trolling can still locate scattered fish, but winter speed and depth control matter.

- Use [Inline Trolling Rig](kb://technique-inline-trolling-rig) or [Flasher Rig](kb://technique-flasher-rig) where appropriate.
- Troll slower than an aggressive warm-water search pace.
- Make turns and watch which speed/depth triggers strikes.
- Target the actual fish marks rather than an assumed bottom depth.

### Streams and Rivers

Cold-water trout seek positions that reduce energy expenditure.

Focus on:

- deep pools;
- slow seams beside faster current;
- pockets behind boulders or wood;
- undercut banks;
- tailouts and soft water;
- tributary mouths where legal and appropriate.

Present through the feeding lane without forcing the fish to chase across heavy current.

## Winter Conditions That Matter Most

### Stable Weather

Cold fish often respond better to stability than to a specific temperature number. A stable period can create a repeatable pattern; a sharp front may reduce activity or move fish a short distance deeper/tighter to cover.

### Sun

Sun can be beneficial in winter because it warms shallow rock, dark bottom, wood, and protected pockets. For bass especially, a sunny afternoon may create the best short feeding window of the day.

### Wind

A hard cold wind can make shallow winter water less attractive and make kayak control unsafe. Moderate wind can still concentrate forage, but in winter **protection and stability** often deserve more weight than they do in fall.

## A Practical Winter Trip Plan

1. **Confirm the water is open and legal to fish.** Seasonal closures and species rules vary.
2. **Start with stable habitat and forage.** For bass, find steep/hard winter structure; for trout, scan the whole mixed water column.
3. **Fish slowly before moving.** Winter fish may need several high-quality presentations through the same zone.
4. **Use sonar deliberately.** Grouped bass or suspended trout are more valuable clues than featureless “good-looking” water.
5. **Recheck a shallower warming area in the afternoon.** Especially after several mild days.
6. **Record the exact depth and structure of every bite.** Winter patterns can be narrow but highly repeatable.

## Cold-Water Kayak Safety

Winter water in Washington can be dangerous even on a calm day. A PFD is essential, and cold-water immersion protection should match the conditions. Keep trips conservative, account for wind and daylight, and do not let a fishing pattern pull you farther from a safe landing than your cold-water plan allows.
