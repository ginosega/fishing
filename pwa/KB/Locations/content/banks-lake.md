Banks Lake is a large, exposed Columbia Basin reservoir with an unusually broad fishery. From a Steamboat Rock State Park base, the best late-September kayak targets are smallmouth bass, walleye, yellow perch, and — when wind is very light and the deep-water setup is practical — lake whitefish. Largemouth bass, rainbow trout, kokanee, crappie, bluegill, channel catfish, and burbot are also present, but they are secondary targets for this trip.

For the broader seasonal pattern, see [Fall Fishing](kb://fall-fishing). For bass location and presentation strategy, see [Bass Behavior and Habitat](kb://bass-behavior-and-habitat) and [Bass Fishing Techniques](kb://bass-fishing-techniques). For surface-fishing decisions, see [Topwater Fishing](kb://topwater-fishing).

## September 24-26, 2026 trip priorities

1. **Smallmouth bass — primary dependable target.** Banks is one of Washington's major smallmouth fisheries, and fall fishing improves as the reservoir cools. Fish rocky shorelines, points, boulder fields, submerged rock, saddles, and the first meaningful break near those features. Use dawn/dusk to search shallow and the middle of the day to work deeper structure.
2. **Walleye — primary change-of-pace target.** WDFW identifies Banks as a major walleye lake and specifically notes Barker Flats adjacent to Steamboat Rock as an important area. Think bottom, slow speed, hard-bottom transitions, and nearby depth changes. The current September 2026 local report says the walleye bite has been slow, so do not sacrifice an entire day waiting for it to happen.
3. **Yellow perch — useful secondary target.** Perch can overlap the same flats, breaks, and offshore schools as walleye. In fall they begin moving offshore, making the fish finder increasingly important.
4. **Lake whitefish — optional specialty target.** Whitefish are the reservoir's most numerous species, and September 2026 reports describe an excellent deep-water bite around the Devils Lake area. This is a specialized bottom fishery in roughly 100 feet of water and should be attempted from the kayak only in very stable, light-wind conditions with adequate weight to stay vertical.
5. **Largemouth bass — protected-bay target.** Look for surviving vegetation, shade, reeds, wood, and protected shallow-to-deep transitions in bays rather than treating them like the rock-oriented smallmouth.
6. **Rainbow trout and kokanee — opportunistic.** Both are present. If the [Humminbird Fish Finder](gear://humminbird-fish-finder) shows suspended fish or bait in safe water, trolling can be worthwhile, but they are lower-confidence targets than bass, walleye, perch, and whitefish for this trip.

## Kayak zones from Steamboat Rock

### Steamboat Rock shorelines, points, and rock transitions

This should be the default water whenever you are unsure what to do. Work the shoreline and nearby structural breaks without making a long open-water commitment.

**Dawn and dusk:** start shallow with the [River2Sea Whopper Plopper 60](gear://river2sea-whopper-plopper-60), [Rebel Pop-R](gear://rebel-pop-r), jerkbait, or a small swimbait. Parallel rocky banks, cross points from several angles, and pay special attention to wind-blown rock if the water remains safely manageable.

**After the surface bite fades:** move outward and use the fish finder to trace the first break, isolated rock, humps, and bait. Start around 8-15 feet, then extend to 15-25 feet if shallow structure is empty. Fish a [Ned Rig](kb://technique-ned-rig), [Drop Shot](kb://technique-drop-shot), football jig, jerkbait, or small swimbait. Do not assume every smallmouth is on bottom; if fish are suspended off rock or following bait, match that depth with a jerkbait or swimbait.

### Barker Flats and adjacent breaks

This is the deliberate walleye session. WDFW's Banks Lake walleye work identifies Barker Flats adjacent to Steamboat Rock as a historically important area. Start by using sonar to find the flat-to-break transition, hard bottom, bait, and fish close to bottom. A productive daytime starting range for walleye is often around 18-25 feet, but follow the marks rather than the number.

With current gear, begin with a slow bottom presentation: a 1/4- to 1/2-ounce jig or football jig with a compact craw or baitfish-profile plastic, or a heavier Ned-style presentation. Keep it near bottom, move slowly, and use short lifts rather than aggressive hopping. If fish are suspended or actively chasing, troll or cast a crankbait such as the [Berkley Flicker Shad 5](gear://berkley-flicker-shad-5) along the contour it can reach.

WDFW lists trolling worm harnesses/slow-death rigs, trolling crankbaits, and vertically jigged blade baits as historically productive Banks Lake walleye methods. A crawler harness/slow-death setup or 3/8- to 1/2-ounce blade bait would therefore be a sensible optional add-on, but neither should be treated as part of current My Gear unless added to the inventory.

**Kayak rule:** do not make Barker Flats an open-water objective merely because it is on the plan. If reaching or returning from the target requires a crossing that no longer looks comfortable, stay on the Steamboat Rock structure and fish bass instead.

### Protected bays and coves

Use protected water as both a largemouth option and the wind fallback. Look for healthy remaining vegetation, weed edges, reeds, shade, isolated wood, and the nearest drop into deeper water. Start with a spinnerbait, [Chatterbait](kb://technique-chatterbait-bladed-jig), small swimbait, or [Wacky Worm](kb://technique-wacky-worm), then slow down around the best cover.

These areas can also hold perch and crappie. If sonar shows a compact school, use the lightest practical drop shot, small jig, or small bait presentation rather than continuing to cast bass-sized moving baits over them.

### Devils Lake / deep whitefish water

A September 4, 2026 local report described a large whitefish fleet in Devils Lake, and a September 11 report described anglers taking excellent numbers by fishing dyed/cured shrimp on bottom in about 100 feet. Small hooks and light bites were emphasized.

Because this is deep, open-water fishing, it is optional rather than a must-do kayak mission. If you choose to try it, launching from **Northrup Point** can reduce unnecessary travel; Washington State Parks says that watercraft access operates 24 hours a day. Use the fish finder to confirm a deep school before committing time.

The light [Pflueger President Spincast Rod](gear://pflueger-president-spincast-rod) is available on this trip because the second angler is not using it, but deep whitefish are more about vertical control than rod power. A small egg/bait hook and cured shrimp are appropriate. The current inventory tops out at 1/2-ounce sinkers; for roughly 100 feet of water, bring heavier sinkers so the rig can stay nearly vertical. If wind or drift prevents vertical control, leave the deep-water fish and return to shoreline structure.

## How to use My Gear

### Spinning setup — finesse and sonar fish

Use the [Daiwa Tatula XT](gear://daiwa-tatula-xt-rod) with the [Daiwa Exceler LT](gear://daiwa-exceler-lt-reel), 15 lb [Sufix 832](gear://sufix-832-15), and an 8 lb [Seaguar InvizX](gear://seaguar-invizx-8) leader for the majority of smallmouth work.

Best jobs:
- Ned rig on rock/gravel transitions and points;
- drop shot on fish marked close to bottom or just above it;
- small swimbait for suspended/roaming bass;
- jerkbait when baitfish are visible and bass are willing to chase;
- light perch presentations.

For the Ned rig, start light and move up only enough to maintain bottom contact. For the drop shot, use the owned [Cylinder Weights](gear://cylinder-weights) and adjust weight to depth/wind. In clear water, keep the leader long enough to separate the visible braid from the lure.

### Baitcasting setup — search, rock, and bigger single-hook baits

Use the [Shimano Zodias ZDC72MHB](gear://shimano-zodias-rod) with the [Shimano 22 SLX DC XT 71HG](gear://shimano-slx-dc-xt-71hg-reel). The 30 lb [PowerPro Super8 Slick V2](gear://powerpro-super8-slick-v2-30) is appropriate for the heavier moving-bait and cover jobs; use the 12 lb [Seaguar InvizX](gear://seaguar-invizx-12) leader where a fluorocarbon leader makes sense.

Best jobs:
- football jig on deeper rock and walleye/smallmouth structure;
- spinnerbait or chatterbait on wind-blown shorelines and protected vegetation;
- crankbait around deflection targets and shallower walleye structure;
- Whopper Plopper on braid when covering water at dawn/dusk; keep the drag reasonable with treble hooks.

### Light combo — panfish and specialty duty

The Pflueger light combo can be dedicated to perch, crappie, or a bait rig while the bass rods remain rigged. Its rod is rated 2-8 lb; verify what line is actually on the reel before relying on it for a deep-water whitefish session.

## Three-day plan

### Day 1 — learn the lake close to camp

**First light:** stay near Steamboat Rock and cover rocky banks/points with the Whopper Plopper 60, Pop-R, jerkbait, or small swimbait. Keep moving until you get a strike, follower, or useful sonar information.

**Midmorning through afternoon:** convert to a structure hunt. Use the Humminbird to mark the first and second breaks, isolated rock, bait, and fish. Alternate a Ned rig and drop shot on the spinning setup with a football jig or crankbait on the baitcaster. Record the exact depth and bottom type of every bite.

**Evening:** return to the best shallow-to-deep rock from the morning and give topwater another serious window. If the surface is dead, throw a jerkbait or swimbait at the same targets before leaving.

**Goal:** finish Day 1 knowing one repeatable smallmouth depth/structure pattern. That becomes the fallback for the rest of the trip.

### Day 2 — walleye experiment, bass fallback

**Early morning:** spend the first low-light window on smallmouth topwater unless the forecast and water are exceptionally favorable for a walleye run.

**Calm-water window:** investigate Barker Flats or an equivalent flat-to-break transition. Use sonar first. Fish close to bottom and slowly; give the walleye experiment a defined block of time rather than donating the whole day to a slow bite. If you find perch on the same structure, downsize and take advantage of the school.

**If walleye do not cooperate:** move back to Steamboat Rock rock/points and resume the Day 1 bass pattern. If wind builds, shift into a protected bay and target largemouth with spinnerbait, chatterbait, wacky Senko, or small swimbait.

**Evening:** fish the best wind-safe smallmouth bank with topwater or a moving bait, then follow missed fish with the spinning rod.

### Day 3 — choose the winning fishery

Use the first two days to choose one of three paths:

- **Bass path:** repeat the best combination of rock type, depth, wind exposure, and presentation from Days 1-2 and spend the day patterning similar water rather than exploring randomly.
- **Walleye/perch path:** revisit the productive flat/break at the same light level and depth. If the marks have moved, search the next break deeper before changing lure families.
- **Whitefish path:** only on a very calm, stable day, use Northrup Point to shorten the approach to deep whitefish water, confirm fish on sonar, and fish bait vertically near bottom. If the lake begins building, abandon the deep-water session early.

Save the final evening for the best bass water of the trip. Late September is exactly when the improving fall bass pattern can produce an excellent last session.

## Wind and kayak decision rules

Banks Lake is large and wind can be the limiting factor. Local paddling guidance specifically warns that strong winds are common in the Grand Coulee, can funnel up or down the reservoir, and can swirl unpredictably around Steamboat Rock.

- Favor **early starts** and shorter routes before afternoon thermal wind develops.
- Stay close enough to a shoreline that beaching is a practical option when conditions are uncertain.
- Treat whitecaps, rapidly increasing gusts, or a difficult upwind return as a reason to leave open water immediately.
- Do not let a fishing waypoint force an exposed crossing. There is productive rock, cover, and protected water close to the park.
- Wear the PFD, carry communication/navigation, and dress for immersion rather than afternoon air temperature.
- Recheck the actual wind forecast each morning and again before any open-water move.

## Electronics pattern

The Humminbird is especially valuable here because the reservoir is too large to fish efficiently by shoreline appearance alone.

1. Idle/paddle a contour before casting it when practical.
2. Mark bait first, then fish, then the exact structural feature holding them.
3. For bass, note rock-to-sand/gravel transitions, isolated boulders, point tips, ledges, and fish suspended just off structure.
4. For walleye, spend extra attention on fish tight to bottom along 18-25-foot breaks and on the deeper edge if that zone is empty.
5. For perch, look for multiple compact marks near bottom rather than a single large return.
6. For whitefish, do not fish 100 feet merely because a report mentioned 100 feet; confirm a school first.

## Regulations and access — checked September 14, 2026

Banks Lake is open year-round and two-pole fishing is allowed. The Banks Lake special rules permit chumming; crappie have a 9-inch minimum and daily limit of 10; yellow perch have no minimum and a daily limit of 25. Other game fish use statewide rules. Current statewide rules include a 12-inch minimum and daily limit of 8 for walleye, with only one over 22 inches; daily limit 10 for smallmouth bass with only one over 14 inches retained; and daily limit 5 for largemouth bass with the statewide slot restriction.

Washington State Parks lists Steamboat Rock State Park hours as 6:30 a.m. to dusk, with Northrup Point watercraft access operating 24 hours a day. The park lists a $7 daily watercraft launch fee. Recheck park notices, lake level/ramp conditions, and the Fish Washington app immediately before the trip.

No Banks Lake-specific emergency fishing rule was listed on WDFW's current emergency-rule page when this plan was researched, but emergency rules can change after this page is written.
