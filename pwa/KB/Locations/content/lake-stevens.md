Lake Stevens is a roughly 1,000-acre, deep (about 150 ft maximum) natural lake east of Everett. It supports smallmouth and largemouth bass, kokanee, stocked rainbow trout, yellow perch, and some other panfish/catfish. It is open to fishing year-round. For a first kayak trip, **focus on bass and perch near a launch** rather than committing to the deep open-water basin. Kokanee become a particularly strong alternative in spring and summer.

**Trip-planning update: October 2026.** The locations and tactics below are starting hypotheses based on access maps, habitat, WDFW species guidance, and historical local angler reporting—not GPS-verified current-day bite reports. Use sonar, bottom type, weather, and actual catches to refine them.

For broader advice, see [Bass Behavior and Habitat](kb://bass-behavior-and-habitat), [Bass Fishing Techniques](kb://bass-fishing-techniques), [Fall Fishing](kb://fall-fishing), [Ned Rig](kb://technique-ned-rig), and [Drop Shot](kb://technique-drop-shot).

## Where to launch the kayak

### North Cove Boat Launch — first choice for a shorter bass/perch trip

- **Access:** 17th Place NE off 124th Avenue NE, downtown Lake Stevens; city address [12200 17th Pl NE](https://lakestevenswa.gov/504/North-Cove-Boat-Launch). There is a two-lane ramp, non-motorized dock, restrooms, and parking.
- **Why start here:** Immediately explore North Cove's protected shorelines, docks, and shallow-to-deep transitions. If conditions allow, work toward the northeast arm, its offshore flat, and the first drop toward deeper lake water. This is an efficient way to learn the lake without crossing the main basin.
- **Parking:** The [city's launch information](https://lakestevenswa.gov/304/Public-Boat-Launch-Information) currently lists $10/day or $5/three hours; a valid WDFW Vehicle Access Pass is accepted **at North Cove only**, as are qualifying city passes. Park in an appropriately marked vehicle space; do not assume you can occupy a trailer-only stall.
- **Hours:** Boat launch opens one hour before dawn and closes one hour after dusk; park access is 7 a.m.–dusk. Avoid fishing in the designated ramp/loading area and do not obstruct launch traffic.

### Davies Beach — west-shore and south-arm alternative

- **Access:** [Davies Beach, 20 S Davies Rd](https://www.lakestevenswa.gov/503/Davies-Beach), at Davies Road and Chapel Hill Road; formerly Willard Wyatt Park. Two-lane ramp, non-motorized dock, restrooms, fishing area/pier, and parking.
- **Why choose it:** A starting point for the west shoreline's dock/weed/rock transitions, the shallower southern arm, and offshore breaks reachable without a full-lake crossing. Better if wind direction makes this shore the protected option.
- **Parking:** Same city fee schedule; the WDFW Vehicle Access Pass is **not** accepted at Davies Beach. Launch hours are one hour before dawn through one hour after dusk.
- **Stay clear** of marked swimming areas, the ramp, and posted no-fishing zones.

**Other shoreline reference:** [Lundeen Park](https://www.lakestevenswa.gov/505/Lundeen-Park), 10020 Lundeen Pkwy on the northwest shore, has shoreline access and a non-motorized dock, but is **not listed by the city as one of the two public boat launches**. Treat it as a landmark / shore-fishing option, not a confirmed trailered or motorized kayak put-in.

**Maps:** [City lake access/boating maps](https://www.lakestevenswa.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/1134) and [Snohomish County's bathymetry/depth-contour maps](https://www.snohomishcountywa.gov/5393/Stevens). Study contours before choosing a route; mark your launch as a return waypoint.

## What to target, by season

| Target | When it is most practical | Where to start | First presentation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Smallmouth bass** | Spring through fall; particularly useful for a first visit | Rocky/gravel banks, points, docks by deeper water, first breaks (about 6–20 ft initially) | Ned rig, jerkbait, 3–4 in swimbait; drop shot deeper |
| **Largemouth bass** | Late spring–early fall; possible in October while water cools | Sheltered cove pockets, vegetation edges, docks, shade, nearby breaklines | Wacky Senko, soft jerk shad, spinnerbait |
| **Yellow perch** | Summer; also catchable in fall/winter as schools shift deeper | Outside weedline and flatter shelves; sonar schools around 10–30 ft as a starting search band | Small drop shot or 1/16–1/8 oz jig with small plastic/worm |
| **Kokanee** | Best local fishery generally May–August | Suspended fish over northeast-arm flat in spring, offshore basin/thermocline in summer | Slow troll small dodger + Wedding Ring, depth matched to sonar |
| **Rainbow trout** | Spring, then nearshore again as fall water cools | Points, edges near launch, and suspended schools offshore | 1/8–1/4 oz spoon, inline spinner, small jerkbait, or slow troll |

WDFW identifies smallmouth and largemouth as dominant warmwater species and calls Lake Stevens a notable kokanee fishery. Yellow perch and stocked trout give you productive alternatives. WDFW also lists black crappie and brown bullhead, but neither is a primary trip objective here.

## Search zones and why they are promising

### 1. North Cove and its mouth — first-stop bass/panfish pattern

From North Cove launch, start by **fishing away from the ramp and swimming/public dock activity**. Follow the cove's permitted shoreline edge. Make casts at isolated dock shade, changes from soft to hard bottom, visible weeds, and the first contour break leading toward the lake.

- **Largemouth:** In warmer months begin roughly 3–10 ft beside weeds/docks; check the outside weed edge or adjacent 10–18 ft if shallow cover looks empty. A [Yamamoto Senko](gear://yamamoto-senko) or [PowerBait Power Jerk Shad](gear://berkley-powerbait-power-jerk-shad) is a good slower presentation.
- **Smallmouth:** Where bottom turns rocky/gravelly and depth drops, use the [Z-Man Ned Rig Kit](gear://zman-ned-rig-kit) or [Z-Man Finesse TRD](gear://z-man-finesse-trd). Start around 8–15 ft, then probe the next break or docks close to deeper water.
- **Perch:** If small sonar marks bunch along the outside vegetation edge or a shelf, stop searching with large bass lures; use a small drop shot with a short plastic/worm and check several nearby depths.

These are **search zones**, not assertions that a particular dock or cove always holds fish. If you get no contact in 20–30 minutes, change depth or bottom type before changing every lure.

### 2. Northeast arm and its 30–50 ft shelf — bass edges / spring kokanee exploration

Historical Lake Stevens contour-based kokanee guidance identifies a comparatively broad **30–50 ft bottom-depth flat in the northeast arm**. That bottom depth is *not* the trolling lure depth. From North Cove, look for the lip where the shallow arm drops toward deeper water.

- **Bass:** Check rocky transitions, protruding points, and any firm-bottom break with bait. Drag a Ned rig or [Drop Shot](kb://technique-drop-shot) on or near the edge; switch to a jerkbait/swimbait if marks are suspended.
- **Kokanee (spring):** Use sonar to locate suspended schools, possibly starting with lures around 10–30 ft below the surface before adjusting to what you mark. Troll a small dodger and compact lure rather than following the bottom at 30–50 ft.
- **Perch:** If marked near bottom along the shelf, downsize and vertical-fish; drift only as fast as you can maintain contact.

Only continue out of the cove while return conditions remain comfortable. Do not mistake the sheltered water inside North Cove for conditions on the open lake.

### 3. West shore from Davies Beach — docks, points, weeds, and contour breaks

From Davies Beach, fish the immediately accessible west-shore structure in one direction, then work back along a different depth band. Look for distinct points, rock/gravel beside docks, underwater slope changes, and weed edges (where present).

- **Smallmouth:** Jerkbait such as the [Rapala Husky Jerk](gear://rapala-husky-jerk) or [Rapala RipStop](gear://rapala-ripstop) over harder bottom, then a Ned rig around 8–20 ft. On hot summer afternoons, let sonar guide you onto deeper 20–30 ft rock.
- **Largemouth:** Senko or spinnerbait around protected vegetation and darker/shaded dock pockets. Fish the *outside* edges if weeds are dying back in fall.
- **Trout/perch:** When the bass bite slows, look for suspended trout off the next contour or schools of perch near bottom.

### 4. Southern arm — shallow shelf transitioning to the main basin

Historical contour guidance notes that the southern arm stays relatively shallow farther offshore, particularly along its west bank. It can be worth probing in spring for roaming kokanee/trout and in the warmer season for bass near cover, but **do not assume all the flat is good fishing**. Look for a specific weed edge, rock/soft-bottom transition, small point, bait concentration, or the break to deeper water.

If the shelf shows no bait or fish, move to the next transition rather than spending time repeatedly crossing featureless water.

### 5. Main basin — deliberate kokanee trip only when conditions and tackle permit

Lake Stevens reaches roughly 150 ft maximum depth. Spring kokanee may be relatively high in the water column; as the lake stratifies in summer, productive fish often move deeper. Historical local reporting describes trolling presentations around **60–90 ft down** in summer (actual depth varies greatly by season/day).

Use your fish finder to distinguish **bottom depth** from **suspended-fish/lure depth**. A light torpedo weight with lots of line out does *not* reliably deliver a dodger/lure to 60–90 ft; that commonly requires a downrigger or purpose-built depth-control setup. Don't plan this as the initial kayak mission if your current setup cannot safely and predictably reach marked fish.

**Obstruction warning:** A historical Northwest Fishing Reports article describes an aeration structure and submerged cable/buoy near the central lake. Treat its precise position as **unverified today**; stay well clear of marked structures/lines, and do not drag downrigger balls near them.

## Exactly what to use from My Gear

### Spinning rod — finesse bass and perch

Use [Daiwa Tatula XT](gear://daiwa-tatula-xt-rod) / [Daiwa Exceler LT](gear://daiwa-exceler-lt-reel) with your 15 lb [Sufix 832](gear://sufix-832-15) and 8 lb [Seaguar InvizX](gear://seaguar-invizx-8) leader.

- **Ned rig:** 1/10 oz mushroom head + 2.75 in [Finesse TRD](gear://z-man-finesse-trd) (or your Ned Rig Kit). Cast past a rock/weed edge, let it settle, then drag/shake lightly with pauses; watch for bites on the fall. Increase weight only when wind/depth prevents contact.
- **Drop shot:** Small finesse bait such as [Z-Man Finesse ShadZ](gear://zman-finesse-shadz), with an owned [cylinder weight](gear://cylinder-weights). Hold it near an offshore break or marked fish; subtle shakes and pauses usually beat constant bouncing.
- **Panfish:** 1/16–1/8 oz jig or compact drop shot with a small plastic or worm. When fish are marked vertically, lower straight to their depth rather than casting blindly.

### Baitcaster — cover water, dock edges, moving fish

Use [Shimano Zodias](gear://shimano-zodias-rod) / [SLX DC XT](gear://shimano-slx-dc-xt-71hg-reel).

- **Search:** [Rapala RipStop](gear://rapala-ripstop), [Husky Jerk](gear://rapala-husky-jerk), [Berkley Flicker Shad](gear://berkley-flicker-shad-5), or a 3–4 in [6th Sense Divine Swimbait](gear://sixth-sense-divine-swimbait). Fan-cast across points and parallel to breaklines; vary retrieve until something follows or strikes.
- **Cover:** [Yamamoto Senko](gear://yamamoto-senko) in dock shade or weed openings; [Power Jerk Shad](gear://berkley-powerbait-power-jerk-shad) near vegetation; [Divine Spinnerbait](gear://sixth-sense-divine-spinnerbait) on breezy/stained banks.
- **Low light:** [Whopper Plopper 60](gear://river2sea-whopper-plopper-60) around safely fishable shallow edges, but do not force a topwater pattern in cold fall water.

### Kokanee/trout trolling — a separate setup

You own the [Luhr Jensen Trout & Kokanee Dodger](gear://luhr-jensen-trout-kokanee-dodger) and [Mack's Wedding Ring](gear://macks-wedding-ring). Start with dodger → approximately 10–18 in leader → small Wedding Ring, optionally tipped with shoepeg corn where legal. Run a speed around **1.0–1.5 mph** to start (a practical test range, not a Lake Stevens guarantee), then adjust until the dodger and lure work properly. Make broad S-turns, mark strikes on GPS, and repeat productive lure depths.

For active trout near the surface or shoreline, cast a [Kastmaster](gear://kastmaster), [Dick Nite spoon](gear://dick-nite-spoon), or small inline spinner. Kokanee are delicate-mouthed; use a light, forgiving drag and a net, and avoid lifting fish on the line.

Your [Humminbird fish finder](gear://humminbird-fish-finder) is particularly useful for finding depth transitions and schools. The [Garmin Force Current](gear://garmin-force-current-kayak-trolling-motor-with-power-steering-foot-pedals) can assist positioning/trolling **once its mount, electrical protection, pairing, and on-water calibration are complete**; do not make the trip contingent on unfinished commissioning.

## First-visit plan: 4–5 hours, fall (October–November)

**Choose North Cove on a calm day; if wind makes the west shore more sheltered, use Davies Beach.**

1. **Launch / first 30 minutes:** Set a return waypoint. Stay within the protected launch-side area, clear of the ramp/swim zone. Fan-cast a jerkbait or small swimbait along the first shoreline and dock/rock transitions. Note water temperature, clarity, wind direction, bottom and bait marks.
2. **Next 60–90 minutes:** Fish the **first firm-bottom / depth transition** (try roughly 8–18 ft) with a Ned rig. Parallel the break, then cast across it. If sonar shows fish deeper, work 15–25 ft with a drop shot. Use several spots rather than anchoring on an empty one.
3. **Midmorning switch:** On marks that look like perch schools, downsize to a small jig/drop shot. If bass bites stop, follow the first break or outside weed edge instead of continuing to pitch empty shallows.
4. **Last hour:** Revisit whichever point, dock line, or break actually produced a bite/follower. If there is no pattern, explore one more distinct bottom/contour transition on the way back. Return before breeze, cold, or traffic becomes limiting.

**Decision rule:** In October, prioritize **smallmouth + perch** and treat largemouth as a protected-cover opportunity. Don't make offshore kokanee your default; the spring/summer fishery is more dependable, and adult kokanee may be moving toward spawning habitat in fall.

## Seasonal adjustments

- **March–April:** On sunny, warming afternoons seek north-end/protected shallows for prespawn bass; keep a jerkbait handy. Trout become an attractive alternative near accessible shoreline and points. Kokanee may be high in the water column; test shallow trolling near the NE arm.
- **May–June:** Excellent window for shallow-to-middle-depth bass, and historically productive Lake Stevens kokanee. Use 55–60 °F water as a cue to inspect smallmouth gravel/rock transitions; do not assume a fixed spawning date. Run kokanee lures at the *marked* fish depth, not the bottom depth.
- **July–August:** Get on early to avoid peak powerboat traffic. Fish bass shallow at dawn/dusk, and check 15–30 ft or deeper structural edges midday. Kokanee often occupy cooler suspended layers offshore; deeper trolling gear and weather margin matter.
- **September–November:** Find surviving weeds, schools of bait/perch, and rock/depth transitions. Start 8–20 ft for bass, adjust to sonar/temperature, and slow your presentation as water cools. Kokanee decline as adults enter spawning season.
- **Winter:** Slow, deeper bass and perch are possible, but cold-water exposure, short days, and wind should dominate kayak decisions. Choose protected access and shorten the route.

## Lake rules, safety, and prelaunch checklist

- **Fishing rules:** [WDFW Lake Stevens](https://wdfw.wa.gov/fishing/locations/lowland-lakes/lake-stevens) says fishing is year-round, chumming is permitted, and this is a two-pole-eligible lake (an endorsement is needed to actually fish two rods). Its lake profile reports a **10-kokanee daily opportunity**. Consult the [current fishing rules/emergency changes](https://wdfw.wa.gov/fishing/regulations) or Fish Washington app for current kokanee, trout, bass, perch, and any special restrictions *on the day you go*; do not use this page alone to decide possession/size limits.
- **Boating:** The [city boating guide](https://www.lakestevenswa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/16068/Rev-2-2026-20230831-External-Final-Lake-Stevens-Parks-Department-Water-Recreation-Policy) says motorboats travel counterclockwise; boats yield to kayaks; no wake within 100 ft of swim areas/structures and marked shore zones; North Cove is no-wake after **1 p.m.** inside its buoy-marked boundary. Expect faster wake-producing traffic elsewhere, especially on summer afternoons.
- **Personal setup:** Wear a PFD, carry whistle/light, phone in drybag, spare paddle, battery reserve, and dry clothing. Keep the motor/prop clear of launch traffic and weeds, and confirm propulsion system protection and kill-stop/MOB function before relying on it.
- **Go/no-go:** Check [forecast/wind](https://forecast.weather.gov/) and on-water conditions. Avoid open-basin crossings if gusts, whitecaps, wake, reduced visibility, or cold exposure compromise a safe return. This is a powerboat lake, not a sheltered pond.
- **Fishing etiquette:** Don't fish in ramps or swim zones; keep outside dock-owner/private-water conflicts, and obey posted shoreline restrictions. Don't anchor across the marked boating route.
