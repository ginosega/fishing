Kayak and bank fishing while camping at Ike Kinswa State Park.

For general presentation strategy, see [Bass Fishing Techniques](kb://bass-fishing-techniques), [Topwater Fishing](kb://topwater-fishing), and [Trout Fishing Techniques](kb://technique-trout-fishing).

Fish:

- Rainbow trout
- Kokanee
- Largemouth bass
- Tiger muskie
- Northern pikeminnow
- Other warmwater/panfish possible; verify before relying on species list

## Morning kayak plan
Target points, shaded banks, and surface rises
- Baitcaster:
  - Lure: Berkley Flicker Shad crankbait rigged with a leader and snap
- Spinning rod: 
  - Lure: Rapala 1/8 oz yellow perch Husky Jerk jerkbait or Rapala RipStop 3-1/2" moss back shiner
  - poons/spinners: 1/8 oz Kastmaster spoon rigged with a leader and snap, or 1/8 oz inline spinner rigged with a leader → swivel → leader → direct tie
  - Technique: With the jerkbait, cast, crank it down a little, twitch-twitch-pause. Pause longer if fish seem inactive.
- Trolling:
  - Rig: `1/2 oz torpedo weight → swivel → dodger → 18–30" leader → Wedding Ring tipped with a Power Egg`
  - Technique: Troll it 70–120 feet behind the kayak, paddle slowly along contour edges, points, and the edges of coves. Make slow S-turns. If you get a strike, repeat same depth and route. Useful if the trout are deeper than spinners and spoons can reach.

## Midday trout setups (10:30 AM to 4 PM)
Explore and troll deeper, fish shade/cover, or scout evening spots.
- Baitcaster:
  - Crankbait: Rapala DT06 3/8 oz demon (Jacob's) rigged with leader and snap. Cast to cover, deflect it off the bottom, wood, or weeds, if it hits grass, pop it free.
- Spinning rod:
  - Lure: Rapala 1/8 oz yellow perch Husky Jerk jerkbait or Rapala RipStop 3-1/2" moss back shiner rigged with leader and snap. Also Kastmaster 1/4 oz or Dick Knight spoon rigged with a snap, or inline spinner rigged with a swivel. Use a spoon when fish are following but not striking, water is calm and clear, or trout seem spooky. Use a spinner around creeks, shaded banks, or rocky edges.
  - Technique: Target docks, laydowns and shoreline wood, overhanging trees, shaded rock banks, weed edges near deeper water, isolated stumps, cove mouths, any shady pocket with 4–10 feet of water nearby, points
- Trolling:
  - Rig: `Leader → 1/2 oz torpedo weight → 3–5 ft leader → lure` 
  - Lure: Berkley Flicker Shad, 1/4 oz Kastmaster spoon, or Dick Nite spoon with Berkley Power Bait trout egg
  - Technique: Troll near points, shelves, cove mouths, outside bends of the old channel, steeper shaded banks, transitions from 10–15' into 20–40'. Run lure 70–120' behind kayak. Make slow S-turns. If not hitting bottom or weeds and fish are deeper, slow down or let out more line. If snagging, shorten the line or paddle faster. 
  - Scouting: On the sonar look for suspended arcs, bait clouds, fish over deeper water, a consistent depth where fish appear. If seeing fish consistently at 15–25', make repeated passes at that depth. Current gear won't reach deeper fish.

## Evening bass setups
Target bass around shoreline cover.
- Baitcaster:
  - Lures: Berkley Power Jerk Shad on 4/0 EWG or 5/0 Owner Twistlock. If there is wind, stained water, or baitfish activity, switch to 6th Sense Divine Spinnerbait with the 6th Sense Divine paddle tail swimbait trailer. If you see thick pads, mats, or surface ambush cover, try the Booyah Pad Crasher Jr. or regular Pad Crasher for topwater fishing.
  - Technique: Fish around docks, laydowns, shoreline wood, shaded banks, over weed tops & edges, coves, points, and shallow cover near deeper water
- Spinning rod:
  - Lure: Yamamoto Senko on weedless wacky hook, or ned rig on harder-bottom points or rocky edges. Also drop shot, Rapala 1/8 oz yellow perch Husky Jerk jerkbait, or Rapala RipStop 3-1/2" moss back shiner crankbait.
  - Technique: Cast to dock shade, outside weed edge, openings in vegetation, shaded banks, wood edges. Cast to the edges of cover and let it fall on slack line. Most bites happen on the fall. 

## Bank fishing
Target trout, perch, and small bass around swimming-area edges, points or corners, shade from overhanging trees,  deeper water close to shore, dock edges, weed edges, wind-blown banks, cove mouths, and areas where you see fish activity.

**Bottom rig**
- Gear: Spincast or spinning rod rigged with `main line → 1/2 oz egg sinker → glass bead → barrel swivel → 18–30" leader → hook with PowerBait dough, nugget, or egg.` Start with a 24" leader and adjust: go longer (30-36") if weeds & muck on bottom, shorter (18") if fish are missing bites: go shorter or strong wind or current. Verify at the shoreline that the hook floats, and add more PowerBait or use a smaller hook if it sinks. 
- Technique: Cast it out, let the sinker hit bottom, reel just enough to remove slack, then set the rod down and watch the tip. Wait 10–15 minutes per cast. If no bite, reel in, check the bait, and recast left/right or farther/closer. Feel for light taps, the rod tip bouncing, line slowly tightening, or the rod loading up steadily. With PowerBait, give the fish a moment, then reel down and lift firmly. 

**Bobber fishing**
- Gear: Spincast or spinning rod rigged with `Main line → bobber → 2–5' of line below bobber → small split shot → hook with small piece of nightcrawler, Power Bait egg, nugget, or small dough ball.` Start with the bait suspended 3–5' below the bobber and shorten if fish are rising or shallow or go longer if no bites and deeper water nearby: 5–8 feet, preferably with a slip bobber. If there are lots of weeds keep the bait above the weed tops.
- Technique: Bobbers work best when placed near an edge or activity. Cast near shade lines, weed edges, dock edges, shoreline drop-offs, visible fish activity, and slightly wind-blown water. Let the rod sit, but occasionally give the bobber a small twitch and let the bait settle again. If the bobber dips, slides sideways, or slowly goes under, reel down and lift.

**Casting from shore**
- Gear: For trout, use 1/4 oz Kastmaster or Dick Nite spoons, Panther Martin 1/8 oz spinners, or Rapala 1/16 oz Original Floating or Huksy Jerk crankbaits. For bass, use a wacky worm, Whopper Plopper, soft or hard jerkbaits, or diving crankbaits over rocky bottoms.
- Technique: Fan cast in a clock pattern: left, straight out, right, then move 20–30 yards down the bank. For spoons and spinners use a steady retrieve with occasional pauses. For jerkbaits, crank down, twitch-twitch-pause. For wacky worms, cast to cover and let them fall.

**Timing**
- Early morning: Still fish with a slip sinker and cast around it with spoons, inline spinners, or jerkbaits.
- Mid-day: This is the toughest time for bank fishing, but try a bottom rig cast to the deepest reachable water, a bobber rig if there is shade, weeds, or visible fish activity, or a wacky worm or jerkbait around shade or cover for bass
- Evening: This is the best time for bank fishing. In the first 30 minutes still fish with a bottom rig and cast around it with spoons, inline spinners, or jerkbaits. In the last light, switch the active rod to a Whopper Plopper, wacky worm, or jerkbait if bass are shallow.
