## Size
Lake whitefish are found throughout much of Canada and parts of the northern United States, including all of the Great Lakes. On average, the lake whitefish weighs only 4 pounds but they commonly reach 20 inches and can grow to 31 inches.

## Location
Fishing for Lake Whitefish peaks during the summer as fish congregate in large schools over the deepest portions of the reservoir. There is a slight lull in October as fish transition to spawning areas. Fishing success peaks again in early to mid-December as fish congregate along the shoreline to spawn. Fish near steep drop-offs or rocky bottoms where whitefish congregate.

### Natural Baits
Lake whitefish primarily feed on bottom-dwelling organisms, including insect larvae, clams, snails, freshwater shrimp, and zooplankton, with larger fish occasionally taking minnows. Effective natural baits include:
- Salmon eggs: Small eggs are ideal due to the whitefish’s small sub-terminal mouth
- Maggots and waxworms: These mimic the insect larvae that whitefish naturally consume in spring and early summer
- Wigglers and small worms: Wire worms or similar small worms, often tipped with maggots, are highly effective, especially in shallower water

When using natural bait, it’s important to match the size to the fish’s small mouth, typically using small hooks (size #14 is recommended) to ensure a proper bite

### Artificial Lures
Artificial lures can also be highly effective, particularly when mimicking natural prey:
- Jigs: Weight-forward jigs (e.g., Ned rigs) allow fishing right on the bottom, stirring up sediment to attract whitefish
- Spoons: White jigging spoons and small Len Thompson Yellow & Red spoons are popular choices

Colors like gold, copper, or red beads often increase attraction, and tipping artificial lures with maggots or small worms can enhance effectiveness

### Seasonal Considerations
- Spring: Whitefish feed heavily on emerging mayfly larvae, making insect-based baits particularly effective
- Fall and Winter: Bottom-feeding baits like clams, worms, and small fish work well, as whitefish feed primarily during daylight hours in shallow-bottomed areas

### Presentation Tips
- Use light to medium-light rods with sensitive lines to detect subtle bites
- Keep bait near the bottom, using small lifts or subtle jigging motions to mimic natural prey movements
