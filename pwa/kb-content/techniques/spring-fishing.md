# Environmental Factors

## Water Temperature
Water temperature is a guide, but spring bass respond strongly to multi-day warming trends, overnight lows, wind direction, sunlight, and water clarity. A sunny afternoon in protected shallow water may fish several degrees “warmer” than the main lake.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Water Temp | Typical Bass Behavior |
| <45°F | Winter mode. Deep, sluggish, hard to pattern. |
| 45–50°F | Smallmouth start becoming catchable; slow finesse presentations dominate. |
| 50–55°F | Prespawn movement begins. Fish begin transitioning shallower, but many still hold on the first deeper break or staging structure. Fish slowly in 8–20 ft near routes to shallow spawning areas, points near spawning coves, rock-to-sand transitions, and emerging weed lines. Best bet is a Ned rig fished slow and deep; drag and dead stick around rock, gravel, docks, and sparse weeds. Also, suspending jerkbait with long (3-8 second) pauses, dropshot, and small swimbaits. |
| 55–60°F | Major improvement. Both largemouth and smallmouth become more active. Start using chatterbaits, spinner baits, and wacky worms. Also, the afternoon is much better than early morning because the water warms several degrees by late afternoon in shallow areas. |
| 60–65°F | Spawn/postspawn peak activity. Often the best overall action. |

## Wind
If there is a light to moderate wind, check wind-blown banks, points, and weed edges. Wind can push baitfish into these areas and make bass less spooky. This is especially useful with jerkbait, small swimbait, chatterbait, and spinnerbait.

## Water Clarity
|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Water Clarity | Best Starting Approach |
| Clear | Ned rig, drop shot, natural jerkbait, small swimbait |
| Light stain | Jerkbait, chatterbait, spinnerbait, wacky worm |
| Muddy/stained | Chatterbait, spinnerbait, darker colors, more vibration |

## Depth
In prespawn, fish the route between winter water and spawning water — not just the spawning flat itself. Focus on the first break, outside weed edge, dock line, point, or rock transition adjacent to shallow flats.

# Gear

## Lures
Use jerkbaits/swimbaits for suspended smallmouth, Ned/drop shot for bottom-oriented fish.

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rod | Primary Bait | Backup |
| Spinning | Ned rig | Drop shot / wacky worm |
| Baitcaster | Suspending jerkbait | Swimbait / chatterbait |

## Colors
- Ned rig: green pumpkin, goby, mud minnow
- Jerkbait: ghost minnow, perch, silver/black, natural shad
- Swimbait: smelt, perch, white/silver
- Chatterbait: green pumpkin, bluegill, black/blue in stained water

## Hooks
Swimbait: Use a 2.8"–3.8" paddle-tail swimbait on:
- 1/8 oz jig head in shallow water
- 3/16 oz jighead for 6–12 ft
- 1/4 oz jighead for deeper edges or wind
