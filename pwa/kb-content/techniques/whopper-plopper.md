A hard-plastic floating lure with a propeller that makes a loud plopping and chopping sound.

### Use

- On windy, choppy days, although the sound will travel more and fish can track it better in calm to lightly rippled water.
- On mornings and evenings.
- In summer through early fall when surface temperatures are high and fish are feeding near the surface.
- Near cover such as wood, docks, and rocky shorelines, and over submerged vegetation.
- Attracts aggressive surface bites, especially from bass, pike, and muskies.
- Good for covering lots of water when searching for fish.

### Gear

- Rod: 7'–7'6" medium-heavy, fast.
- Reel: Baitcaster, medium speed (6.4:1–7.5:1).
- Line: 40–50 lb braid, moss color.
- Knot: Tie direct to the lure with a [Palomar knot](kb://knot-palomar). It is okay to use a small snap if changing lures a lot.

### Technique

- Cast far out and reel in with a slow, steady retrieve.
- Occasionally speed up or pause to trigger followers and allow the sound to vary in volume and pitch.

### Bait

- Larger sizes (110, 130, 190) can handle faster retrieves and call in fish from a distance, while the 75/90 sizes are better for finesse or small water.
- River2Sea Whopper Plopper 110.
- Berkley Choppo 105.
