## Spinning

Use spinning gear when:

- You’re fishing light baits (wacky worms, drop shots, Ned rigs, small jerkbaits, inline spinners).
- You need to cast very light lures far.
- You’re fishing clear water and want to use 6–10 lb test fluorocarbon for stealth.
- You’re fishing windy conditions; spinning reels handle wind better, with no backlash.
- You’re vertical jigging or fishing from a kayak, where easy one-handed control helps.

Typical spinning combo:

- **Rod:** Medium-light to medium power, fast action, 6'6"–7'2".
- **Reel:** 2500–3000 size spinning reel.
- **Line:** 10–15 lb braid + 6–10 lb fluorocarbon leader.

## Baitcasting

Use baitcasting gear when:

- You’re throwing heavier lures (spinnerbaits, chatterbaits, crankbaits, swimbaits, topwaters).
- You’re fishing thick cover (weeds, wood, docks) and need to horse fish out.
- You’re targeting larger fish with heavier line (15+ lb fluorocarbon or 30+ lb braid).
- You need pinpoint casting accuracy—skipping under docks or hitting tight shoreline targets.
- You want instant power transfer on hooksets.

### Technique

- **Set the brake:** Use the dial indicator inside the body for the type of line: mono (M), fluoro (F), braided (P). This defines how restrictive the brakes will be—P is least restrictive and F is the most. You don’t have to match your line type; this essentially gives you 15 different brake settings.
- **Set the cast control/spool tensioner knob:** With the lure attached, hold the rod tip up and press the clutch lever. The lure should drop to the ground with medium controlled speed.
- **Set the brake dial:** W (wind) or 4 for lightweight lures or casting into wind; 3 or lower for heavier lures and calm conditions.
- Thumb the spool at splashdown to avoid backlashing.

## Why Use Both

Because each excels in different conditions:

- Spinning tackle handles finesse presentations that baitcasters can’t throw effectively. A 1/8 oz Ned rig will backlash on a baitcaster, for example.
- Baitcasting tackle provides control and torque for reaction baits and power fishing. A 1/2 oz chatterbait or 3/4 oz jig needs the muscle of a baitcaster.

In short:

- **Spinning = finesse & light precision**
- **Baitcasting = power & control**

## Three Rod Quiver

| Situation | Setup to Use | Example Lure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear, calm water – fish are shy | Spinning | Wacky worm, drop shot, Ned rig |
| Cold front, slow bite | Spinning | Small swimbait or finesse worm |
| Moderate cover or cloudy day | Baitcasting (general) | Spinnerbait, chatterbait, crankbait |
| Active fish chasing bait | Baitcasting (general) | Whopper Plopper, walking bait, chatterbait |
| Heavy weeds or lily pads | Baitcasting (heavy) | Frog, buzzbait |
| Docks and structure | Baitcasting (heavy) | Heavy jigs |
