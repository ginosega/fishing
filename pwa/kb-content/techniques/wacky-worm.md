A soft plastic worm hooked crosswise through the middle of the body, leaving both ends free to wiggle as it falls, imitating an injured baitfish or worm.

# Use
- Primarily a topwater fishing technique, although you can let It sink
- Best for finicky or pressured bass that won’t chase fast-moving lures
- Good in spring to early fall, especially around the spawn or post-spawn, and calm or sunny conditions when fish are spooky
- Use in clear to lightly stained shallow (2-8') water around shallow cover, docks, grass edges, and visible fish you can sight-cast to
- If it's not hitting or you want to fish deeper in the water, try a Ned rig

# Gear
- Rod: Medium power, fast action
- Reel: Spinning
- Line: 10 lb. braid or fluorocarbon
- Hook: Size 1 – 1/0. Use VMC crossover rings in the center of the worm vs. putting the hook directly through the worm. These are wider than O-rings, which can cut through the worm.
- Knot: Tie directly to leader with a Palomar knot
- Soft plastic worm such as 5" Yamamoto Senko
- Color: Green pumpkin w/black flecks for clear water, black w/blue flecks for stained or muddy water.

# Technique
- Cast to a spot, pause for a second or two, then give the line three quick tugs to wiggle the worm and pause again. Do this 5 times and if no hits the reel in and try again in a different area.
- In deeper (>5') water you can pause it for 5-6 seconds to let it sink. It will wiggle as it does.
- Don't set the hook too early - wait until you feel the full weight of the fish
