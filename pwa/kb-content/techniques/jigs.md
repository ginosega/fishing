Hook with attached
weighted head, rubber skirt, and soft plastic trailer.

# Standard/Casting/"Arky" Jig

- 1/2 oz. round or
  oblong-shaped head, stands on its back. Vertical line tie is better for
  grass, horizontal for wood and rocks.
- Use: Shallow to medium-depth
  (<10') water, around and under structure: dock, rocks, wood, and along
  weed lines
- Gear: 7'+ medium heavy or
  heavy fast rod,  20-lb. fluoro line.
- Knot: Improved Clinch with
  the tail tucked back through the line tie
- Technique: Let the lure sink
  to the bottom after landing, giving it extra line to do so. Hop it off the
  bottom while varying the retrieve speed, pausing occasionally.
- Set the hook hard (thick wire
  gauge)

# Swim Jig

- 3/8-1/2 oz. pointed head,
  does not stand on its back
- Use: Shallow (up to 3')
  water, in and around grasses, heavy cover, and docks
- Gear: 7'+ medium heavy or
  heavy fast rod, 30-lb. braid
- Technique: Start retrieving
  right after the cast lands. Keep lure suspended at or near the surface
  while retrieving. Pop and pulsate the rod tip to flare the skirt.

# Football-Head Jig

- 3/4 oz. oblong heavy head
  that sinks to bottom, displacing water and banging off structure
- Use: in deep (10'+) water
- Gear: 7'-6" heavy fast
  rod, 17-20 lb. fluoro
- Technique: Pull out line
  after landing so allow the lure to sink to the bottom. Slowly drag the jig
  back while keeping contact with the bottom. Goal is to bang the head off
  rocks and structure while kicking up a cloud of dust.
- Set the hook hard (thick
  gauge wire)

# Finesse Jig

- Small (<3/8 oz.) round or
  mushroom-shaped head with smaller hook and short skirt
- Use: In and around rocks in
  cold water or pressured fish when bait fish are small. See [Ned Rig](kb://technique-ned-rig).
- Gear: 7' medium-heavy fast
  baitcasting or spinning rod
- Technique: Let the lure sink
  to the bottom after landing, giving it extra line or leaving the bail open
  to do so. Gently shake the rod tip to hop the bait on the bottom
  interspersed with slow retrievals to walk it toward you. Do not use a hard
  hook set (thin gauge wire).

# Weed Guards

- Unless fishing in heavy
  cover, look for lighter-weight guards - makes it easier to set the hook

# Trailers

- Use craw or paddle-tail
  plastics that match the jig color
- If using a crawfish trailer,
  rig it horizontally to imitate a craw and to prevent jig from getting
  hooked up on wood if it falls over. Can rig vertically when using a swim
  jig.
- Use a chunk craw trailer for
  cold water or pressured fish; the action is minimized vs. a rage craw

# Color

- Rule of thumb is to use white
  or silver in clear water, green pumpkin in stained water, and black with
  blue flake in muddy water
- Jig and trailer combinations:
  go for contrast and use more red. Red and black, red and green, green and
  black, etc.
