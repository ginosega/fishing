## Lake Washington

### General

Common user targets: bass and coastal cutthroat trout. Kokanee can be present/seasonal in some contexts but should be treated as a specialized target and regulations should be checked.

### Spring notes from OneNote

Lake Washington is more of a smallmouth, structure, transition-zone, electronics/sonar lake than Lake Sammamish. Fish are often deeper and more spread out. Smallmouth may suspend off structure rather than sit directly on bottom, so jerkbait or small swimbait can outperform bottom baits when fish are roaming.

### Juanita Bay

- Launch: Juanita Bay Park.
- Better largemouth-oriented spring area because it is shallower with more vegetation and warmer, protected water.
- Use chatterbaits, weightless Senkos, spinnerbaits; frogs later in year.

### North end / Kenmore / Log Boom / Sammamish River mouth

- Launch: Kenmore Boat Launch for kayak.
- Dock fishing: Log Boom Park public dock has been verified in prior chat as allowed, subject to signage/current rules.
- Fish Arrowhead Point sides and 6-15 ft transition zones, north shoreline flats, and Sammamish River mouth.
- Spring: suspending jerkbait, Ned rig, small paddletail swimbait; go slow with long 3-8 second pauses.
- Later spring/warmer water: chatterbaits, spinnerbaits, topwater.

User observation:

- Evening kayak trip near weed edges/channel where Sammamish River meets lake: wacky worm and spinnerbait from 8:30-9:30 p.m.; small fish rising for flying insects; no bites. Future adjustment: subtler baitfish/finesse, fish outside break or predator zones.

### Kirkland waterfront

- Launch: Kirkland Marina Boat Launch.
- Fish rocky points, steep banks, marina edges, docks adjacent to depth.
- Use suspending jerkbait or small swimbait for suspended smallmouth; also drop shot and Ned rig.

### Mercer Island

- Launch: Mercer Island Boat Launch.
- Classic prespawn smallmouth territory with steep structure, rocky transitions, 10-25 ft staging, steep drop-offs.
- Fish east-side rocky banks, I-90 bridge riprap, west-side docks and transitions.
- Use suspending jerkbait/small swimbait, drop shot, Ned rig.

## Lake Sammamish

### General spring positioning

Lake Sammamish is smaller and more manageable than Lake Washington, warms faster, has more vegetation, and largemouth are generally easier to find in spring. Prespawn fish transition between winter staging areas and emerging weed edges, shallow warming flats, and docks adjacent to deeper water. North-end shallows often warm before the main basin.

### North end

- Best spring area due to shallow warming water, Sammamish River influence, vegetation, docks, protected water, largemouth concentration.
- Launch: Idylwood Park.
- Fish afternoon on warm sunny days, starting on outside weed edge or first break, then moving shallower as afternoon warms.
- Targets:
  - isolated docks adjacent to weed edges and 6-12 ft darker-bottom areas;
  - emerging weed edges/new growth/submerged grass in 4-10 ft;
  - river-mouth transition where current meets lake and shallow meets basin.
- Use jerkbaits, swimbaits, finesse rigs.

### South end

- Launch: Lake Sammamish State Park.
- Can be excellent later in spring; early prespawn is timing-dependent.
- Fish edges of weed flats near deeper water and docks near Issaquah Creek.

### East shoreline

- Steeper banks, docks, transition zones, scattered rock.
- More smallmouth-oriented, similar to Lake Washington.
- Use jerkbait around rocky transitions, points, and dock edges; Ned rig around rock-sand transitions; small 3-4" swimbaits.

### User observation / catch

- 2026-08-04: 10" perch and 12" largemouth at Lake Sammamish; kayak fishing over a bench west of Tibbetts Beach on south end; caught in <10 ft along edge of cover on Strike King KVD Square Bill 1.0 crankbait.
- Historical observation: possible smallmouth spawning/juvenile area about half mile north of State Park launch on east side, around 12 ft flat dropping on three sides; larger fish may stage on adjacent deeper edges.

## Mayfield Lake / Ike Kinswa State Park

Context: user camped/planned camping at Ike Kinswa State Park with kayaks.

Discussed fish:

- Rainbow trout
- Kokanee
- Largemouth bass
- Tiger muskie
- Northern pikeminnow
- Other warmwater/panfish possible; verify before relying on species list

### Morning kayak plan

- Gino/baitcaster: Flicker Shad 5 Pearl White; troll contour edges/points/cove mouths.
- Trolling option: Wedding Ring tipped with Power Egg behind 1/2 oz torpedo weight, dodger, and 18-30" leader.
- Jacob/spinning: 1/8 oz Kastmaster, Panther Martin, Husky Jerk, RipStop; cast to points, shaded banks, cove mouths, surface rises, or troll 50-90 ft behind kayak.

### Midday kayak plan

- Trout: deeper trolling with 1/2 oz torpedo weight + Flicker Shad/Kastmaster/Dick Nite.
- Bass: shade/wood/docks/outside weed edges with Power Jerk Shad, wacky Senko, jig + Rage Craw, DT06.
- Scout evening spots with Navionics/Helix.

### Evening kayak plan

- Bass around cover: soft jerk shad, spinnerbait, Whopper Plopper 60, wacky Senko, DT06.
- Trout if rising/active: Kastmaster, Dick Nite, Panther Martin, small jerkbaits.

### Mayfield catch observations from OneNote

- 2026-08-19: 12" northern pikeminnow at Mayfield Lake; kayak fishing off point at southern end of Ike Kinswa State Park; caught in 20 ft along drop-off using Berkley Flicker Shad 5 crankbait.
- 2026-08-20: 12" northern pikeminnow at Mayfield Lake; kayak trolling off west shore of Ike Kinswa State Park; caught in 25 ft over shelf using Berkley Flicker Shad 5 crankbait.

### Bank fishing at Ike Kinswa

Targets: trout, perch, small bass around swimming-area edges, points/corners, shade, deeper water close to shore, dock edges, weed edges, wind-blown banks, cove mouths, visible fish activity.

Bottom rig:

`spincast/spinning rod -> main line -> 1/2 oz egg sinker -> glass bead -> barrel swivel -> 18-30" leader -> hook with PowerBait dough/nugget/egg`

Bobber rig:

`main line -> bobber -> 2-5 ft line below bobber -> small split shot -> hook with nightcrawler piece, Power Egg, nugget, or dough`

Casting from shore:

- Trout: Kastmaster, Dick Nite, Panther Martin, Rapala Original Floating, Husky Jerk.
- Bass: wacky worm, Whopper Plopper, hard/soft jerkbaits, diving crankbaits over rocky bottoms.

## Lake Chelan

OneNote notes:

- Fish early morning/evening, dawn/dusk.
- Fish boat-launch docks, rocky shoreline, points, riprap, shade lines, and quick drop-offs.

Smallmouth gear:

- Ned rig: green pumpkin, 1/10-1/6 oz.
- Drop shot: 3-4" shad/minnow/worm bait.
- Tube jig: green pumpkin or smoke/purple, dragged slowly.
- Small paddletail: 2.8-3.3" on 1/8-1/4 oz jighead.
- Small jerkbait or topwater first/last light.

Trout gear:

- 1/8-1/4 oz Kastmaster or Little Cleo-style spoon.
- Small inline spinner.
- Slip-sinker rig with PowerBait or worm for relaxing bite chance.
