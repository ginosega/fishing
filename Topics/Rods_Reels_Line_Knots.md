## When to use spinning vs baitcasting

### Spinning

Use spinning gear when:

- fishing light baits such as wacky worms, drop shots, Ned rigs, small jerkbaits, inline spinners;
- needing to cast very light lures far;
- fishing clear water with 6-10 lb fluorocarbon leader;
- fishing windy conditions where avoiding backlash matters;
- vertical jigging or kayak fishing where easy one-handed control helps.

Typical spinning combo:

- Rod: Medium-light to medium power, fast action, 6'6"-7'2".
- Reel: 2500-3000 size spinning reel.
- Line: 10-15 lb braid + 6-10 lb fluorocarbon leader.

### Baitcasting

Use baitcasting gear when:

- throwing heavier lures such as spinnerbaits, chatterbaits, crankbaits, swimbaits, topwaters;
- fishing thick cover such as weeds, wood, docks;
- targeting larger fish with heavier line;
- needing pinpoint casting accuracy;
- wanting stronger hookset power/torque.

## Baitcaster setup notes

For Shimano SLX DC XT:

- OneNote linked a [Baitcaster Reel Setup & Casting](https://youtu.be/YDlL1rP7FdU?si=VFgCGySj94xqgCwU) video.
- Internal line-type dial: mono (`M`), fluoro (`F`), braid (`P`). This defines braking range; it does not have to match line type exactly and effectively provides 15 brake ranges/settings.
- Spool tension/cast-control knob: with lure attached, hold rod tip up and press clutch. Lure should drop with medium controlled speed.
- External brake dial: `W`/4 for lightweight lures or wind; 3 or lower for heavier lures and calm conditions.
- Thumb spool at splashdown to avoid backlash.

## Line material notes

### Braid

- [Braided-line setup and spooling video](https://youtu.be/Vd2qcWQBW54?si=RHgL76r3M1T-LaUy).
- Use with fluorocarbon leader for most clear-water/finesse/general-purpose work.
- Braid cuts vegetation, lasts longer, and transmits feel; leader adds invisibility and abrasion resistance.
- Bright braid is useful when a leader is used because it improves strike visibility.
- Moss braid is useful for direct topwater/heavy cover applications.
- Use 8-strand braid for general purpose casting smoothness; 4-strand can be tougher in abrasive environments.
- Be careful on hooksets because braid has no stretch; lift and reel rather than violently snapping the rod.

### Fluorocarbon

- Nearly invisible and sinks.
- Good in clear water, deep structure, cold water, and slow retrieves.
- Not good for topwater because it sinks.
- Wet knots before tightening.
- Brands noted: Seaguar InvizX, Sufix Advance.

### Monofilament

- Floats and stretches.
- Good for topwater and for shallow/rocky/snaggy cover where some shock absorption helps.
- Less ideal for deep lures where stretch reduces sensitivity and floating line reduces depth.
- Brands noted: Trilene XL, Sufix Elite.

| Setup | Preferred knot | Easier field knot | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 lb braid to 8 lb fluoro | FG knot | Albright/Alberto | Spinning/finesse; 6-8 ft leader common |
| 30 lb braid to 12 lb fluoro | FG knot | Albright/Alberto | Baitcaster; 4-7 ft leader common; avoid burying bulky knots deep in spool |

## Line-type knot reference

### Braided

- To leader: FG knot, or Albright when on the water.
- To swivel: N/A; the swivel is in the leader.
- To snap: Palomar.
- To hook: N/A; use a leader.
- To lure: Palomar or Modified Uni for direct topwater fishing; otherwise use a leader.

### Fluorocarbon

- To swivel: Trilene.
- To snap: Trilene.
- To hook: Improved Clinch or Palomar for heavier tackle.
- To lure: use a snap for more action; otherwise Palomar.
- Moisten before tightening to prevent friction from weakening the line.

### Monofilament

- To swivel: Improved Clinch.
- To snap: Improved Clinch.
- To hook: Improved Clinch or Palomar for heavier tackle.
- To lure: use a snap for more action; otherwise Palomar.

## Direct tie / snap / swivel guidance

| Lure type | Connection | Swivel use |
|---|---|---|
| Crankbaits | Leader to small crankbait snap or direct | No swivel normally |
| Hard jerkbaits | Leader to snap or direct to split ring | No swivel normally |
| Spoons | Leader to small snap | Swivel optional if twisting/rolling |
| Inline spinners | Direct tie or snap after leader | Ball-bearing swivel in/above leader preferred |
| Wedding Ring / trolling spinner | Direct to rig/lure | Swivel strongly preferred |
| Spinnerbait | Direct tie | No snap/swivel; hardware can interfere with wire arm |
| Chatterbait | Direct tie | No snap/swivel; can disrupt blade startup/vibration |
| Wacky/Ned/drop shot | Direct tie | No snap/swivel except drop-shot rig hardware if desired |
| Topwater | Usually direct tie or small snap depending lure | Avoid fluorocarbon leader for floating topwater if it pulls bait down |

## Snaps and swivels

### Snaps

- Snaps have a weight rating; use the right weight snap for the line.
- Use a snap to enable switching between multiple lures, or when the bait has a tie point with edges.
- Use a snap with fast-moving lures that you will be retrieving quickly or trolling with.
- Do not use a snap with live or jig bait that you work slowly, because this can make the presentation look unnatural. Tie the line directly to these lures instead.
- Do not use a snap with a weedless presentation because it can snag on weeds. Tie the line directly to the hook instead.
- Do not use snaps or swivels with floating/topwater bait when the extra weight could cause it to sink.

### Swivels

- Use a swivel to prevent line twist when using a spinning lure such as a spoon, spinner, or flasher, or a vertical jigging lure that swims in a circle.
- Ball-bearing swivels are much better than barrel swivels; use them for connecting lures and leaders when using monofilament and when spinning or trolling.
- A barrel swivel is okay for sinker rigs or when connecting a monofilament leader to braided line, although a direct line-to-line knot such as an FG is preferable when practical so the swivel cannot damage the guides.

### Snap swivels

- Avoid connecting snap swivels directly to the lure because they add extra weight and can look unnatural.
- They may be acceptable with spinners and spoons that you will change frequently because those lures already have substantial hardware on them.

## Trout rigging examples

### PowerBait bottom rig

OneNote still-fishing rig; see also [Slip sinker rig](https://www.wired2fish.com/tackle-tips/how-to-tie-a-slip-sinker-rig):

`main line -> 1/2 oz egg sinker -> bead -> swivel -> leader -> #4 hook with dough or nugget`

Open conflict: prior chat guidance often suggested #8 hooks for PowerBait/Power Eggs. Both are preserved; see TODO.

### Bobber bait rig

OneNote bobber rig:

`main line -> bobber -> leader -> split shot -> swivel -> leader -> #4 hook with piece of worm run up hook shank to eye`

### Inline spinner rig

If using mono/fluoro mainline:

`mainline -> swivel -> 12-18" leader -> optional snap -> inline spinner`

If using braid + leader:

`mainline -> 12-18" leader -> swivel -> 12-18" leader -> optional snap -> inline spinner`

### Kayak trolling weight rig

OneNote linked [Trout trolling rigs](https://sportfishingbuddy.com/trout-trolling-rigs/).

Inline rig:

`main line -> torpedo weight with swivels at each end -> 3-4' leader -> Wedding Ring / hoochie / small spinner, with Power Egg`

Flasher/dodger rig:

`main line -> small sliding weight -> bead -> swivel -> 3-4' leader -> dodger -> 18-30" leader -> Wedding Ring / hoochie / small spinner, with egg`
