## Regulation discipline

Before fishing a waterbody, check:

- Fish Washington app / current WDFW rules.
- Emergency rules.
- Species-specific rules.
- Bait rules and retention implications.
- Selective gear / barbless / catch-and-release restrictions.
- Park, dock, and launch signage.

Do not rely indefinitely on old notes for current regulations.

## Keeping fish from a kayak

Basic process for a legal fish intended for harvest:

1. Net the fish and keep the kayak balanced.
2. Confirm species, legality, size, and limit.
3. Dispatch quickly with a firm blow to the head.
4. Bleed the fish.
5. Chill it quickly in a cooler/fish bag or otherwise keep it cold.

## Bleeding a trout or similar fish

1. Bonk/stun/kill the fish first.
2. Open one gill plate.
3. Cut or snip the red gill arches on one side; both sides bleed faster.
4. Hold fish in water briefly by net/stringer to let blood drain.
5. Put fish on ice or in cooler/fish bag.

Kayak safety: do not lean far over the side; keep hooks controlled and weight centered.

## Fish identification notes

### Kokanee

General lake-phase clues:

- bright silver sides;
- blue-green/dark back;
- deeply forked tail;
- small soft mouth;
- usually no obvious black spots on body or tail.

Distinguish from trout and juvenile salmon carefully; check regulations before keeping.

### Northern pikeminnow

General clues:

- long slender body;
- large mouth;
- forked tail;
- silvery/olive back and pale sides;
- yellowish fins.

User caught northern pikeminnow at Mayfield trolling Berkley Flicker Shad 5 on 2026-08-19 and 2026-08-20.

### Bullhead catfish

Typically small catfish, often 8-14 inches, occasionally larger. Often use shallow to moderate muddy/silty areas, weeds, wood, coves, and night/low-light feeding periods.

### Tiger muskie

May be present in Mayfield Lake. Treat as a toothy trophy species requiring careful handling and current regulation checks. Do not intentionally target without appropriate leader, pliers, and release tools.

## Dock/bank etiquette

- Do not cast around swimmers, boaters, paddlers, or people launching craft.
- Respect posted signs.
- Keep tackle and rods organized so treble hooks do not become a hazard.
- Use a net and pliers/hemostats.
- Pack out line and bait containers.
