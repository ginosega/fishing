A long and slender topwater lure designed to move in a zig-zag pattern. It stays on the surface and imitates a struggling or fleeing baitfish, often with internal rattles. Good for largemouth and smallmouth bass, striped and hybrid bass, and pike.

# Use
- In calm or slightly rippled water where fish can see and track the lure
- In low light (morning/evening) when predators feed near the surface
- In warmer months (late spring to early fall) when fish are active and hunting shallow baitfish
- Around cover such as docks, weed edges, and points

# Gear
- Rod: 6'6"–7' medium or medium-heavy, fast action for quick twitches
- Reel: 7:1 or faster baitcaster or spinning reel to pick up slack quickly
- Line: 30–40 lb braid or 12–17 lb mono—both float
- Avoid fluorocarbon, as it sinks and ruins the walking action
- Knot: Tie directly to the eye or split ring using a [loop knot](kb://knot-loop-non-slip-loop). It is okay to use a small snap if you’re changing baits often, but avoid snap-swivels, as they add weight and hinder motion.

# Technique
- Cast long, past the target area
- Point the rod tip down toward the water
- Twitch the rod with a short downward snap, pause and give the line slack to allow the bait to glide, then reel in the slack. Repeat rhythmically
- The bait should glide left-right-left-right. If you pull too tight it’ll swim straight; a bit of slack is what makes it “walk”
- Vary speed and cadence until you find what triggers strikes

# Color
- Bone or white on cloudy days
- Translucent or shiny colors on sunny days; also black
