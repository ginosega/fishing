# Snaps
- Snaps have a weight rating - use the right weight snap for the line
- Use a snap to enable switching between multiple lures, or when the bait has a tie point with edges
- Use a snap with fast-moving lures that you will be retrieving quickly or trolling with
- Don't use a snap with live or jig bait that you work slowly, as this will make the presentation look unnatural. Tie the line directly to these lures instead.
- Don't use a snap with a weedless presentation, as it will snag on weeds. Tie the line directly to the hook instead.
- Don't use snaps or swivels with floating/topwater bait, as the extra weight can cause it to sink

# Swivels
- Use a swivel to prevent line twist when using a spinning lure (spoons, spinners, flashers) or a vertical jigging lure that swims in a circle (jigging rap).
- Ball-bearing swivels are much better than barrel swivels; use for connecting lures and leaders when using monofilament and when spinning or trolling. Okay to use a barrel swivel for sinker rigs or when connecting a mono leader to a braided line (although it's better to use an FJ knot for that so that the swivel does not damage the guides)

# Snap Swivels
- Avoid connecting snap swivels directly to the lure, as it adds extra weight and looks unnatural. May be okay to use with spinners and spoons that you will be changing these out frequently because they already have a lot of hardware on them anyway.
